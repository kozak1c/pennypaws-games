Plush Hour Privacy Policy

Penny Paws Games · Effective November 14, 2026

The short version

  • Plush Hour has no accounts, no sign-in and no chat.
  • We (Penny Paws Games) run no servers and keep no information about you. Your shops are saved on your device and are never sent to us.
  • The game shows video ads from Google AdMob only when you choose to watch one for a reward. To show ads, Google collects information about your device and how you use the game, and the ads may be personalized where the law allows.
  • You can buy packs of gems through Google Play. Google handles the payment; we never see payment details.
  • Postcards and Move my shop are codes that you copy or send yourself, with the apps you choose.
  • Plush Hour is made for a general audience and is not meant for children under 13.

This policy covers the Plush Hour app for Android, installed from Google Play.

What we collect

Nothing ourselves. We run no servers that receive information from the game, and the game has no account or sign-in. It does not ask for or collect email addresses, phone numbers, photos, contacts or precise location, and it does not use the camera or microphone. The only information that leaves your device on its own is what Google's ad and purchase software sends to Google, described below. Besides vibration, the game asks only for the Android permissions that software needs: internet access, network status, the advertising ID and Google Play billing.

What stays on your device

The game saves your shops on your device so you can pick up where you left off: your progress, items, coins and gems, how your rooms are decorated, the story scenes you have seen and the replies you picked, the postcards you have received, your settings, how many ads you have watched that day, and the dates and times the game uses for its timers and daily events. It also keeps a record of the gem packs it has added to your shop.

The game asks you to type a name for your shopkeeper. You can skip it. The name is kept in your saved shop and shown in the game's story scenes and on your shop sign. It leaves your device only if you send a Move my shop code (see below). Please use a nickname rather than your full name.

This information is never sent to us. The game switches off Android's backup to your Google account for its data. If you set up a new phone by copying data straight from your old phone, Android may copy the game's saved shops along with your other apps.

To erase a shop, open Settings, tap Erase this shop and confirm. This erases the shop you are playing (the game has three shop slots, which you can switch between in Settings). Uninstalling the game deletes everything it saved in its own storage, including any copies it kept for safety.

Postcards

You can send postcards to friends who play Plush Hour. A postcard is a code made of letters and numbers. When you send one, the game copies a message with the code to your phone's clipboard and opens Android's share sheet, where you choose the app and the person to send it to. That app handles the message under its own privacy policy. We are not involved and never see it.

A postcard code carries only game details: your shop's name and sign (picked from the game's own lists), your level, how your rooms are decorated, a few game records, a greeting picked from the game's list, where a gift is hidden, the date it was sent, and a random number the game made for your shop so your friend's game can tell your postcards apart. A thank-you postcard also carries the random number of the shop it thanks. A postcard does not carry the name you typed for your shopkeeper.

To open a postcard, you copy the message and tap Paste it in the game's post office. The game reads your clipboard only when you tap Paste it, and uses only the postcard code it finds there.

Move my shop

Move my shop, in Settings, turns your whole shop into a code so you can bring it into the game on another device. Copy all my shops does the same for every shop slot. The code holds everything the game saves for that shop, including the name you typed for your shopkeeper. The game copies it to your clipboard and opens the share sheet, and you decide where it goes. Anyone who has the code can read it and bring your shop into their own copy of the game, so send it only to yourself or to someone you trust. To bring a shop in, you copy its code and tap Bring a shop in; the game reads your clipboard only when you tap that button.

Shop pictures

Share my shop makes a picture of one of your rooms with your shop's name, its level and a few game records. It does not include the name you typed for your shopkeeper. The game saves the picture on your device, in a Plush Hour folder inside your Pictures folder (or in the game's own storage if that does not work). Other apps on your device, such as your photo gallery, can see pictures in the Pictures folder, and the pictures stay there if you uninstall the game. You can share or delete them like any other picture.

Ads (Google AdMob)

Plush Hour is free and shows ads from Google AdMob, and from no other ad network. The ads are optional videos: an ad plays only when you choose to watch one for a reward, such as extra energy.

The game is not set up as an app for children, so AdMob may use your device's advertising ID and may show personalized (interest-based) ads where the law allows. Where the law requires your consent for this, such as in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the game asks for it through Google's consent message before personalized ads are shown, and you can change your choice at any time from the game's Settings. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID, or turn off ad personalization, in your device's settings (on most Android phones, under Settings, then Privacy or Google, then Ads).

To show ads, measure them and prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads software in the game sends Google information such as the device's advertising ID and other device identifiers, the device's IP address and the approximate location Google works out from it, the device model, operating system and language, the app's version, which ads were shown, watched or tapped and other interactions with ads, and diagnostic information such as crash and performance data. Google uses this information for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. Google's list is at developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure. Google uses this information as described in Google's Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services, and keeps it for the periods described there. We do not receive this information; Google shows us only totals, such as how many ads were shown and what they earned.

Purchases (Google Play)

You can buy packs of gems, the game's premium currency, as optional purchases made through Google Play. Google processes the payment under its own terms and privacy policy. We do not see or store names, email addresses or payment details. The game only receives Google Play's confirmation that a purchase was made, so it can add the gems to your shop. Google Play may ask for your Google account password or fingerprint before a purchase.

Children

Plush Hour is made for a general audience. It is not directed at children and is not meant for children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has sent us personal information, for example by email, contact us and we will delete it. A parent can also reset or delete the device's advertising ID in the device's settings, as described above.

When you contact us or test the game

If you email, call or write to us, we use what you send only to answer you. If you test early versions of the game on Google Play, we hold the email address of the Google account you test with, because Google Play uses a list of addresses to decide who may install test versions, and we see any feedback you choose to send us through Google Play. We use these only to run the tests.

Google Play and Android

Google Play and Android may collect information such as install counts and crash reports under Google's own policies and your device settings. Google shows us aggregated reports, such as install counts and crash reports by device model and Android version. Google also shows us ad counts and earnings, and order numbers with country and amount for purchases and refunds. We do not use these reports to identify anyone.

Selling and sharing

We do not sell personal information for money. Letting Google use the information its ad software collects to show personalized ads may count as "sharing" for cross-context behavioral advertising under California law, and as targeted advertising under the laws of some other states. You can opt out by deleting your advertising ID or turning off ad personalization in your device's settings, or by contacting us.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example, in the European Union, the United Kingdom or California), you may have rights to access, correct or delete personal information, and to object to or opt out of its use for advertising. We do not hold personal information from the game, and what it saves is on your device and under your control. For information Google holds, use the tools in your Google account and your device's ad settings. You can still contact us with any request and we will respond.

Where EU or UK law applies, Penny Paws Games is the controller for the ad information collected in the game, and Google also uses it as a controller for its own purposes under its own terms. We rely on your consent for personalized ads, and on our legitimate interest in funding a free game and preventing fraud for other ads. Google transfers data to the United States under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses. We are also the controller for personal information you send us, such as an email, or a tester's email address, which we handle on our legitimate interest in answering you and running tests. You may complain to your data protection authority (in the UK, the ICO).

Changes to this policy

When we change how the game handles information, we will update this page and its effective date before the change reaches players.
