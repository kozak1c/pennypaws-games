Giggle Bomb Privacy Policy

Penny Paws Games · Effective 14 November 2026

The short version

  • Giggle Bomb is a party game for one phone that a family passes around. It has no accounts, no sign-in, no chat, no online play and no way for players to share anything.
  • The game sends nothing to us, and we (Penny Paws Games) keep no information about you or your child from the game. The only information the game sends off the device is technical information the Google ad software sends to Google, and what Google Play needs to sell or restore the Pass, both described below.
  • Players' names, voice recordings, the dares you write and game progress are saved only on your device. We never receive them.
  • The game uses the microphone only when a player taps to record their name or a yell.
  • The game shows ads from Google AdMob, never during a round. Every ad request is marked as coming from a child, which tells Google not to personalize the ads and to keep them to content rated for general audiences.
  • The optional Giggle Pass is bought through Google Play. Google handles the payment; we never see payment details.
  • Buying the Pass and the game's link to this policy sit behind a grown-up check.

This policy covers the Giggle Bomb app for Android, installed from Google Play.

Who we are

Giggle Bomb is made by Penny Paws Games, the trade name of Corey Kozak, an individual developer in the United States, who is the operator of the game. If you have a question about this policy or your child's privacy, you can reach us by email at pennypawsgames@gmail.com, by phone at +1 586-929-9644, or by mail at 4806 Rockport Dr, Durham, NC 27703, USA.

If you email, call or write to us, we keep your message and contact details only to answer you and keep a record of the request.

What stays on your device

The game saves these things on your device so your family can pick up where you left off:

  • The players you make. For each one, the game keeps the name typed for them, their animal and colour, whether they get kid or grown-up questions, what Sparky (the game's announcer) calls them, and the silly voice and loudness picked for their recordings. Sparky calls a player by a nickname picked from the game's list or by their own recording; he does not read out typed names. We suggest a first name or a nickname rather than a full name.
  • Voice recordings, if a player makes any (see the next section).
  • The dares your family writes yourselves, a Giggle Pass feature.
  • Gems, unlocked games and dare packs, things bought in the gem shop and who unlocked them, best scores, the last champion, who is sitting at the table, and your settings.
  • Whether the Giggle Pass is owned, and how many double-gems videos were watched today, which the game uses for its daily limit.

The game never sends this information to us. The game switches off Android's backup for its data, so it is not copied to your Google account. If you set up a new phone by copying directly from your old one, Android may copy the game's saved data, including names and recordings, to the new phone. That copy goes from phone to phone, not to us or to your Google account.

You can delete what the game saved:

  • To remove a player, tap PLAY!, then tap ✏️ Edit and the player's name, then tap the bin button 🗑️. This deletes the player and their recordings.
  • To delete one recording, open the player's card, tap 🎤 My voice, pick My name or Splat yell, and tap the bin button 🗑️.
  • To delete a dare you wrote, open Settings (⚙️), tap 🎭 Dares and tap ✖ next to the dare.
  • Uninstalling the game deletes everything it saved.

The game has no single button that deletes everything at once. You can also delete everything at once in Android's settings: Apps, Giggle Bomb, Storage, Clear storage. A Pass you bought comes back with Restore.

The microphone and voice recordings

A player can record their own name, so Sparky calls them by it, and a short “splat yell” that plays when they go boom. Recording is optional. Everything else in the game works without it, and a player can pick a nickname from the game's list instead.

Recording happens only in the game's voice booth (on a player's card, tap 🎤 My voice), and only after the player taps the red button. The microphone turns off by itself after a few seconds (about 3 for a name, a little longer for a yell), or sooner if the player taps to stop. The first time, Android asks whether the game may use the microphone. If you say no, recording stays off and the rest of the game works as usual. You can change this later in your phone's settings. The microphone is the only permission the game asks you to allow.

Each recording is saved as a sound file in the game's private storage on the device. The game keeps the original and a copy changed to the silly voice the player picks. Recordings are only played back on the device, in the game. The game never sends them to us or to anyone else. The game does not analyse voices or try to recognise who is speaking; it only changes how a recording sounds.

To record again, tap 🎤 Say it again in the voice booth. To delete a recording, tap the bin button 🗑️ there. Removing a player deletes their recordings too.

What we collect

Nothing ourselves. Giggle Bomb does not ask for email addresses, phone numbers, photos, contacts or your precise location (Google's ad software can tell roughly where a device is from its IP address; see Ads), and it does not use the camera. Players type names and may record their voices, as described above, but these are saved on the device and we never receive them. The game has no analytics or tracking tools of our own, and the game sends nothing to us.

How information is protected

The game's saved data and recordings sit in the app's private storage, which Android keeps apart from other apps. The Google ad software sends its information to Google over encrypted connections.

Ads (Google AdMob)

Giggle Bomb is free and shows ads from Google AdMob to players who do not have the Giggle Pass. Ads never play during a round. There are two places they can appear:

  • After a match, a player can choose to watch a video to double the gems from that match. The offer appears at the end of a match, and the game plays no more than a few of these videos a day.
  • A short ad break can appear after a dare is over, before the game moves on. The game spaces these breaks out and has none in solo games.

If no ad is ready, the game simply carries on. The game asks for ads to start with their sound off. The Giggle Pass removes all ads. The Google ad software is still part of the game, so it can still contact Google when the game starts, even on a device that owns the Pass.

Every ad request from the game is marked as directed to children under the U.S. Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and as coming from a user under the age of consent in Europe, and is limited to ads rated for general audiences. With these settings we instruct Google not to show personalized (interest-based) ads or remarketing and to limit the information it uses, as Google describes in its child-directed ads documentation. The game does not request the Android advertising ID permission, or Android's Privacy Sandbox permissions for ad topics and ad measurement.

To show ads, count them and prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads software in the game sends Google technical information: the device's IP address (which can show roughly where the device is, such as the city or region), device model, operating system and language, the app's version, when the game was opened, which ads were shown, watched or tapped, performance information (such as start-up time, freezes and battery use), and an app-level ID Google uses to count ads and prevent fraud (not the advertising ID). Google's list is at developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure. Google uses this information as described in Google's Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services. We do not receive this information. The game never gives the ad software players' names, recordings or dares.

If you see an ad that seems wrong for children, email us the date, the approximate time and what it showed, and we will report it to Google and block the advertiser.

Purchases (Google Play)

The Giggle Pass is an optional one-time purchase made through Google Play. Google processes the payment under its own terms and privacy policy. We do not see or store names, email addresses or payment details. The game only receives Google Play's confirmation that the Pass was bought, so it can unlock it and restore it on a new device. Each time the game starts, it asks the Google Play app on the device for the Pass's price and whether the Google account on the device owns it.

Buying the Pass goes through a grown-up check first, and Google Play may ask for your Google account password or fingerprint as well. Restoring a Pass you already bought does not need the check, because it buys nothing.

Outside links

The game's only link that leaves it goes to this policy, and it sits behind the same grown-up check wherever it appears. Ads can be tapped. A tapped ad opens the advertiser's page or Google Play outside the game, and that is not behind the grown-up check.

Children's privacy

Giggle Bomb is made for children and the grown-ups who play with them. A child can type a name, write a dare (with the Giggle Pass) and record their voice in the game, for the game's own use on the device. That information is saved on the device and is not sent to us or to anyone else. The game gives children no way to share personal information. The only personal information collected from children through the game is collected by the ad software of Google LLC: persistent identifiers (the device's IP address and an app-level ID), together with the technical information listed under Ads. It is collected only to support the game's internal operations: showing ads that are not based on a player's interests or past activity, limiting how often an ad is shown, counting the ads shown and tapped, and detecting fraud and security problems. We rely on COPPA's support-for-internal-operations exception for this, so we do not ask for a parent's consent. To keep this information from being used to contact a particular person, to build a profile of them or for behavioural advertising, the game marks every ad request as child-directed, which tells Google to switch off interest-based advertising and remarketing; the game removes the advertising ID permission, so that ID cannot be read; and the game gives the ad software nothing else about players.

Because we hold no information about your child from the game, there is nothing for us to review or delete. We keep no personal information from the game, so we have no retention period for it. Google keeps the technical ad information for the periods described in its privacy policy. Names, recordings, dares and progress saved on the device can be deleted by you at any time as described above. You can stop any further collection by the ad software by uninstalling the game. While the phone is offline the ad software cannot send anything to Google, and the game still works. If you have questions or concerns, email us and we will help.

Google Play and Android

Google Play and Android may collect information such as install counts and crash reports under Google's own policies and your device settings. Google shows us aggregated reports (install counts, crash reports by device model and Android version, ad counts and earnings, and order numbers with country and amount for refunds). We do not use them to identify anyone.

Selling and sharing

We do not sell personal information and do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, as California law defines those terms.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example, in the European Union, the United Kingdom or California), you may have rights to access, correct, delete or object to the use of personal information. We do not hold any personal information about you from the game, and the data on your device is under your control. For information Google holds, use the tools in your Google account. You can still contact us with any request and we will respond.

Where EU or UK law applies, Penny Paws Games is the controller for the ad information collected in the game, and Google LLC (USA) processes it under its own terms. Our legal basis is our legitimate interest in funding a free game with non-personalized ads and in preventing fraud. Google says it relies on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses for transfers to the United States. You may complain to your data protection authority (in the UK, the ICO).

Changes to this policy

When we change how the game handles information, we will update this page and its effective date.
