Plush Hour Privacy Policy

Penny Paws Games · Effective November 27, 2026

The short version

  • Plush Hour has no accounts, no sign-in and no chat.
  • We (Penny Paws Games) run no game servers, and the game sends us nothing about you. Your shops are saved on your device and are never sent to us.
  • The game shows video ads from Google AdMob only when you choose to watch one for a reward. It asks Google for non-personalized ads. Google's ad software still collects some information about your device to show ads, count them and prevent fraud, described below.
  • You can buy gems and other in-game items through Google Play. Google handles the payment; we never see payment details.
  • Postcards and Move my shop are codes that you copy or send yourself, with the apps you choose.
  • Plush Hour is made for a general audience and is not meant for children under 13.

This policy covers the Plush Hour app for Android, installed from Google Play. You can open it from the game at any time: Settings, then Privacy policy.

Plush Hour is made by Penny Paws Games, the trade name of Corey Kozak, an individual developer in the United States. If you have a question about this policy, you can reach us by email at pennypawsgames@gmail.com, by phone at +1 586-929-9644, or by mail at 4806 Rockport Dr, Durham, NC 27703, USA.

What we collect

Nothing ourselves. We run no servers that receive information from the game, and the game has no account or sign-in. It does not ask for email addresses, phone numbers, photos, contacts or precise location, and it does not use the camera or microphone. The only name it asks for is an optional name for your shopkeeper, which is kept on your device (see below). The only information that leaves your device on its own is what Google's ad and purchase software sends, and the backup Android may make to your own Google account, both described below.

Besides vibration, the game asks for the Android permissions that Google's ad and purchase software needs: internet access and network status, the advertising ID and Android's own ad services (ad ID and ad measurement), Google Play billing, and keeping the device awake and running short background tasks, which come with Google's ad software. If you turn on reminders, the game also asks for Android's notification permission and for permission to set its reminders again after the phone restarts. Reminders are scheduled on your phone by the game; nothing is sent to us or anyone else to deliver them, and you can turn them off in Settings or in Android's settings.

What stays on your device

The game saves your shops on your device so you can pick up where you left off: your progress, items, coins and gems, how your rooms are decorated, the story scenes you have seen and the replies you picked, the postcards you have received, your settings, counts of the ads you have watched, and the dates and times the game uses for its timers and daily events. Your saved shop also notes what your purchases added, such as whether it has the Starter Pack, which gem packs it has had before and how long its Shop Helper stays.

So that a purchase is never given to you twice, the game also keeps a short list of the Google Play purchase codes it has already filled (at most 400). This list is in its own file on your device, not in your saved shop, and the game never sends it anywhere.

The game asks you to type a name for your shopkeeper. You can skip it. The name is kept in your saved shop and shown in the game's story scenes and on your shop sign. It leaves your device only inside a Move my shop code that you send (see below), or inside Android's backup of your saved shops. Please use a nickname rather than your full name.

This information is never sent to us. If backup is turned on in your device's settings, Android may include the game's saved files (your shops and their safety copies, the list of purchase codes and a few small settings files) in your device's backup to your own Google account, and bring them back when you reinstall the game or set up a new device. Google keeps that backup under your Google account; we cannot see it. You can turn backup off or delete it in your device's backup settings. If you set up a new phone by copying data straight from your old phone, Android may copy the game's saved shops along with your other apps.

To erase a shop, open Settings, tap Erase this shop and confirm. This erases the shop you are playing (the game has three shop slots, which you can switch between in Settings). It does not remove shop pictures (see below), the list of purchase codes, or the safety copies the game keeps of damaged or replaced shops. Uninstalling the game deletes everything it saved in its own storage, including those copies and the list of purchase codes. A backup in your Google account is not removed by erasing a shop or uninstalling; delete it in your device's backup settings.

Postcards

You can send postcards to friends who play Plush Hour. A postcard is a code made of letters and numbers. When you send one, the game copies a message with the code to your phone's clipboard and opens Android's share sheet, where you choose the app and the person to send it to. That app handles the message under its own privacy policy. We are not involved and never see it.

A postcard code carries only game details: your shop's name and sign (picked from the game's own lists), your level, how your rooms are decorated, a few game records, a greeting picked from the game's list, where a gift is hidden, the date it was sent, and a random number the game made for your shop so your friend's game can tell your postcards apart. A thank-you postcard also carries the random number of the shop it thanks. A postcard does not carry the name you typed for your shopkeeper.

To open a postcard, you copy the message and tap Paste it in the game's post office. The game reads your clipboard only when you tap Paste it, and uses only the postcard code it finds there.

Move my shop

Move my shop, in Settings, turns your whole shop into a code so you can bring it into the game on another device. Copy all my shops does the same for every shop slot. The code holds everything the game saves for that shop, including the name you typed for your shopkeeper. The game copies it to your clipboard and opens the share sheet, and you decide where it goes. Anyone who has the code can read it and bring your shop into their own copy of the game, so send it only to yourself or to someone you trust. To bring a shop in, you copy its code and tap Bring a shop in; the game reads your clipboard only when you tap that button.

Shop pictures

Share my shop makes a picture of one of your rooms with your shop's name, its level and a few game records. It does not include the name you typed for your shopkeeper. The game saves the picture on your device, in a Plush Hour folder inside your Pictures folder (or in the game's own storage if that does not work). Other apps on your device, such as your photo gallery, can see pictures in the Pictures folder, and the pictures stay there if you uninstall the game. You can share or delete them like any other picture.

Ads (Google AdMob)

Plush Hour is free and shows ads only from Google AdMob. The ads are videos that play only when you choose to watch one for a reward: more energy, popping a bubble, skipping a wait, doubling a daily gift, or an extra Not today for a customer. The game never shows an ad you did not ask for. So that a video is ready when you ask, the game fetches one ahead of time: Google's ad software contacts Google when the game starts and from time to time while it is open, even if you never watch an ad. Ads are limited to content rated for teens and younger. The game is not marked as directed to children.

Every ad request from the game asks Google for non-personalized ads. Google describes these as ads that are not based on your past behavior; they are chosen using the app and your general location, such as your city. Even so, Google's ad software still uses your device's advertising ID or other identifiers to limit how often you see the same ad and to count ads, as Google explains in Personalized and non-personalized ads, and to prevent fraud.

At start-up, Google's ad software checks with Google whether a consent message is required where you are. Where Google's rules require one, the game shows Google's consent message at start-up and loads no ads until you have answered it; if the message cannot be shown, no ads load until it can. Settings then has an Ad privacy choices button where you can change your choices. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your device's settings (on most Android phones, Settings, then Privacy or Google, then Ads).

To show ads, count them and prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads software in the game sends Google information such as the device's advertising ID and other device identifiers, the device's IP address and the approximate location Google works out from it, the device model, operating system and language, the app's version, which ads were shown, watched or tapped, and diagnostic information such as crash and performance data. Google uses this information for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. Google's list is at developers.google.com/admob/android/privacy/play-data-disclosure. Google uses this information as described in Google's Privacy Policy and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services, and keeps it for the periods described there. This information is sent over encrypted connections. The videos come from advertisers: when one plays, or when you tap it, the advertiser and the ad companies Google works with may also receive technical information such as your device's IP address, and a tap opens the advertiser's page or app listing, which has its own privacy policy. We do not receive this information; Google shows us only totals, such as how many ads were shown and what they earned.

Purchases (Google Play)

You can buy optional in-game items through Google Play: packs of gems (the game's premium currency), a Starter Pack, and a Helper Week that adds Shop Helper days. Google processes the payment under its own terms and privacy policy. We do not see or store names, email addresses or payment details. From Google Play's record of each purchase, the game uses only which item was bought, how many, whether the payment has gone through, and the purchase code, so it can add what you bought to your shop. Google Play may ask for your Google account password or fingerprint before a purchase.

Children

Plush Hour is made for a general audience. It is not directed at children and is not meant for children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has sent us personal information, for example by email, contact us and we will delete it. If your child under 13 has played, uninstalling the game removes what it saved in its own storage (shop pictures stay in the Pictures folder until you delete them), and you can reset the device's advertising ID. A backup in your Google account is not removed by erasing a shop or uninstalling; delete it in your device's backup settings.

When you contact us or test the game

If you email, call or write to us, we use what you send only to answer you. If you test early versions of the game on Google Play, we hold the email address of the Google account you test with, because Google Play uses a list of addresses to decide who may install test versions, and we see any feedback you choose to send us through Google Play. We use these only to run the tests.

Google Play and Android

Google Play and Android may collect information such as install counts and crash reports under Google's own policies and your device settings. Google shows us aggregated reports, such as install counts and crash reports by device model and Android version, and ad counts and earnings. For purchases, Google Play shows us order records such as the order number, the date, the item, the price, the device model and the buyer's city, state or region, postal code and country, which we use only for refunds, accounting and taxes. We do not use these reports to identify anyone.

Selling and sharing

We do not sell personal information for money. The only information the game sends to another company on its own is what Google's ad and purchase software sends, as described above, and the backup Android may make to your own Google account (see What stays on your device). The game asks Google for non-personalized ads, which are not based on your activity in other apps or on websites. Google may still collect information about your device over time and across the different apps that use its ad software, as its policies linked above describe. Apps do not receive a browser's Do Not Track signal, so the game does not respond to one.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example, in the European Union, the United Kingdom or California), you may have rights to access, correct or delete personal information, and to object to its use. The game sends us no personal information, and what it saves is on your device and under your control. The little we do hold (messages you send us, testers' email addresses and Google Play's order records) is described above; you can ask us about it or ask us to delete it, though we keep order records as long as tax rules require. For information Google holds, use the tools in your Google account and your device's ad settings. You can still contact us with any request and we will respond.

Where EU or UK law applies, Penny Paws Games is the controller for the ad information collected in the game, and Google also uses it as a controller for its own purposes under its own terms. Where the law requires consent before ads use your device's identifiers, the game asks for it through Google's consent message, and you can change your choice in Settings, Ad privacy choices. Otherwise, our legal basis is our legitimate interest in funding a free game with non-personalized ads and in preventing fraud. Google transfers data to the United States under the frameworks described in Google's Privacy Policy. We are also the controller for personal information you send us, such as an email, or a tester's email address, which we handle on our legitimate interest in answering you and running tests. You may complain to your data protection authority (in the UK, the ICO).

Changes to this policy

When we change how the game handles information, we will update this page and its effective date.
